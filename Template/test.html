A and B working together can complete a piece of work in 12 days. B and C together can finish the same work in 15 days. If A is twice as efficient as C, in how many days can B alone complete the entire work?

Standard LCM Method & Efficiency Breakdown

1. Total Work Calculation: Let total work units = LCM of 12 and 15 = 60 Units.

2. Efficiency of $(A + B) = 60 / 12 = \mathbf{5\text{ units/day}}$.

3. Efficiency of $(B + C) = 60 / 15 = \mathbf{4\text{ units/day}}$.

4. Subtracting $(B + C)$ from $(A + B)$: $(A + B) - (B + C) = A - C = 5 - 4 = \mathbf{1\text{ unit/day}}$.

5. Given that $A$ is twice as efficient as $C$ ($A = 2C$), therefore: $2C - C = 1 \implies C = 1\text{ unit/day}$.

6. Consequently, $A = 2(1) = 2\text{ units/day}$.

7. Efficiency of $B = (B + C) - C = 4 - 1 = \mathbf{3\text{ units/day}}$.

8. Time taken by $B$ alone to complete entire work = $\text{Total Work} / \text{Efficiency of } B = 60 / 3 = \mathbf{20\text{ days}}$.

⚡
AIR 72 Speed Trick Whenever $A = 2C$, difference of pairwise rates directly gives $C$'s isolated efficiency in 4 seconds flat.
